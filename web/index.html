READ THE ASSUMPTIONS

A comparison, with clear boundaries

One horizon, no invented future rate

Compare up to eight user-entered fixed-rate offers on one start date and one end date. The horizon cannot exceed the shortest quoted term. Remaining amortization sets the payment size; it is not a promise that the quoted rate lasts until payoff. There are no current lender offers, market-rate feeds, or affordability approvals.

Canadian semiannual compounding

For nominal annual rate j (as a decimal) and f payments per year, the equivalent period rate is (1 + j/2)^(2/f) − 1. We calculate a monthly annuity over the entered amortization and round it to cents. Standard twice-monthly, biweekly and weekly amounts are monthly × 12 ÷ 24, 26 and 52 respectively. Accelerated biweekly is monthly ÷ 2; accelerated weekly is monthly ÷ 4. Each is rounded to cents. An entered quoted base payment overrides this estimate.

Dates and rounding matter

Monthly payments fall on start-date anniversaries; month-end starts stay at month end. Twice-monthly payments fall at the midpoint (rounded down to a day) and next monthly anniversary. Weekly and biweekly payments fall every 7 and 14 days. A year may contain 53 weekly dates or 27 biweekly dates. The first payment is after the start date. Confirm actual first-payment dates with your lender; this app cannot reproduce a different lender calendar.

Between payment dates, interest compounds in proportion to the days elapsed in that scheduled payment period. Interest rounds to cents at every cash event and at the comparison end. On the same day, the base payment applies first, then recurring extra, then lump sums. The closing amount includes accrued interest even if no payment is due. These explicit conventions are estimates; lender statements can differ.

Fees, cashback and prepayment privileges

Upfront fees and your entered lender-quoted penalty are paid at the start. Financed fees are added to the debt and accrue interest. Cashback kept is received at the start and reduces cost; include a known clawback in charges. No unknown penalty is estimated. Borrowing cost = interest + all entered fees/penalties − cashback kept. Net cash outlay = actual debt payments + upfront fees/penalty − cashback kept.

An annual lump-sum dollar cap is checked in loan-anniversary years, not calendar years. A recurring-extra cap is checked against the base payment. Blank means unknown; zero means none allowed. These amount checks do not confirm permitted dates, number of payments, original-principal bases, approval or other contract rules. Over-limit payments remain in the model with warnings. Ask your lender for an actual charge; this app does not invent an interest-rate differential.

Outside this model

Variable or adjustable rates, taxes, insurance premiums, qualification rules, investment returns, time value of money, refinancing costs not entered, business-day shifts, and unknown penalties or cashback clawbacks. A lower modelled cost is not a recommendation.
